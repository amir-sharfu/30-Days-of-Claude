Claude has broad creative abilities — from writing complete stories to helping you polish a single sentence. Here's what it can do:

## 💡 Brainstorming & Idea Generation

Claude excels at generating large numbers of ideas quickly — ideal when you're stuck or want to explore options.

### Story Ideas

```
Give me 10 original short story premises in the genre of 
psychological thriller. Each one should have:
- A one-line hook
- The central tension or mystery
- An unexpected twist element

Make them varied — different settings, protagonists, and themes.
```

---

### Title Options

```
I've written a novel about a marine biologist who discovers that 
a newly identified deep-sea creature is the source of an ancient 
myth about sea monsters. 

Generate 15 possible titles. Mix approaches: evocative, 
mysterious, clever wordplay, and direct.
```

---

### "What If" Scenarios

```
Generate 8 "what if" scenarios that could form the basis of 
a science fiction story. Focus on near-future technology 
(20–50 years from now) and human consequences.
```

## 📖 Writing Stories

### Short Story from a Premise

```
Write a 600-word short story based on this premise:

A lighthouse keeper discovers that the light she's been 
maintaining for 20 years doesn't guide ships — it's been 
signalling something beneath the ocean.

Genre: literary fiction with elements of quiet horror.
Tone: melancholy and atmospheric.
End on an ambiguous note that leaves the reader unsettled.
```

---

### Opening Hooks

```
Write 5 different opening sentences for a story about 
a disgraced lawyer returning to their hometown after 10 years. 
Vary the technique:
1. Start in the middle of action (in medias res)
2. Start with a provocative statement
3. Start with a sensory description
4. Start with dialogue
5. Start with an intriguing question
```

---

### Scene Writing

```
Write a tense scene between two estranged siblings meeting 
for the first time in 5 years at their father's funeral. 
Neither wants to fight in public, but there's enormous 
unresolved conflict beneath the surface. Show the tension 
through subtext — what they don't say matters as much as 
what they do.
```

## 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Character Development

Strong characters are the heart of memorable stories. Claude can help you build them from the ground up.

### Character Profile

```
Create a detailed character profile for a morally ambiguous 
protagonist in a crime novel:

- Name, age, appearance
- Background and formative experiences
- Core motivation (what do they want?)
- Core fear (what do they avoid?)
- Fatal flaw that will create story conflict
- Speech patterns and quirks
- What they would never do — and what would make them do it anyway
```

---

### Giving Characters a Distinct Voice

```
The following three characters need to respond to the same 
situation. Write their responses to show how different 
their voices are.

Situation: Their boss has just cancelled a project they 
spent 6 months on.

Character 1: Optimistic and pragmatic, always looks for opportunity
Character 2: Bitter and cynical, expects to be let down
Character 3: Conflict-avoidant, suppresses their feelings publicly
```

---

### Character Interview

```
I'm writing a character named Elena, a 34-year-old forensic 
accountant who is secretly running her own fraud investigation 
into her employer. 

Interview her in character. Ask her 10 questions to help me 
understand how she thinks, speaks, and rationalises her choices. 
She should feel like a real person — contradictory and complex.
```

## 🌍 Worldbuilding

Creating fictional worlds is where Claude's ability to generate consistent, layered detail really shines.

```
Help me build the world for a fantasy novel. The setting is 
a city built on a series of ancient stone bridges over a 
mile-deep canyon. The city has been there for 1,000 years 
and nobody knows what's at the bottom.

Give me:
1. The political structure of the city
2. The main social classes and their relationship to height 
   (top of bridges vs. underneath)
3. The mythology around the canyon
4. One unique cultural tradition that developed because of 
   the unusual geography
5. The central tension that would drive a story set here
```

## ✒️ Poetry

### Writing Poems in Different Forms

```
Write a poem about grief using three different forms:
1. A haiku (5-7-5 syllables)
2. A free verse poem (no constraints, emotional and raw)
3. A sonnet (14 lines, ABAB CDCD EFEF GG rhyme scheme)
```

---

### Matching a Style

```
Write a poem in the style of Mary Oliver — focused on 
nature, observation, and what small things reveal about 
life. The subject: a single tree in a city park in winter.
```

---

### Collaborative Poetry

```
I'll write the first stanza, and you write the second. 
Then I'll write the third, and you write the fourth. 
We're writing a poem about the feeling of leaving a city 
you've lived in for many years.

My first stanza:
The boxes are stacked by the door now,
corners of books pressing through cardboard skin.
I expected sadness but find instead
a flat white space where memory used to live.
```

## 🔧 Improving Your Own Creative Work

Claude is excellent at giving targeted feedback on writing you've already done.

### Prose Feedback

```
Read this paragraph and tell me:
1. Where the prose is strongest
2. Where it's weakest and why
3. Any specific words or phrases that feel flat or clichéd
4. What the dominant tone is, and whether it's consistent

[paste your paragraph]
```

---

### Pacing Issues

```
I feel like this scene is dragging. Read it and tell me:
- Where the pacing slows down and why
- What I could cut without losing anything important
- Suggestions for tightening it

[paste scene]
```

---

### Dialogue Check

```
Read this dialogue exchange. Does it feel natural? 
Do the two characters sound distinct from each other?
What would make it more immediate and believable?

[paste dialogue]
```

## 🔄 Genre & Style Experiments

Use Claude to explore unfamiliar styles and push your creative boundaries:

3. Romance (emotionally intense, character-focused)
4. Horror (atmospheric, building dread)

## 🏋️ Exercises

### 🟢 Level 1 — Beginner

1. Ask Claude to write a 300-word short story based on a one-line premise you invent. Read it and note what you like and what you'd change.
2. Give Claude the first sentence of a story and ask it to write 5 different continuations in 5 different genres. Which one would you most want to read?
3. Ask Claude to write 3 versions of the same poem (haiku, free verse, and rhyming). Which form works best for your chosen subject?

### 🟡 Level 2 — Intermediate

4. Create a detailed character using the profile template above. Write a scene featuring that character. Then ask Claude for feedback on whether the character feels consistent and believable.
5. Use collaborative poetry: write alternating stanzas with Claude on a topic of your choice. After 4 stanzas each, reflect on how the collaboration changed the poem's direction.
6. Take a piece of your own creative writing and ask Claude for targeted feedback. Act on at least 3 of the suggestions. Does the piece improve?

### 🔴 Level 3 — Challenge

7. Use Claude to build a complete short story in stages: premise → character profiles → worldbuilding → outline → scene by scene drafts → final edit. Document your prompts. What decisions did you make vs. what did Claude contribute?
8. Experiment with style mimicry: ask Claude to write in the style of 5 different authors you admire (without copying their actual text). Can you identify which stylistic elements Claude captured and which it missed?
9. Research: What is the current debate around AI and creative authorship? Who owns work created with AI assistance? How are publishers, literary magazines, and competitions responding? Where do you stand?
